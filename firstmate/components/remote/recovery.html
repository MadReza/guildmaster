Recovery rules

The remote area has one governing rule for failure: a lost or ambiguous SSH call means "completion unknown", so the route is preserved, nothing is blindly repeated, and an unreachable remote endpoint is reported as unknown rather than dead, and is never replaced locally.

Read against Firstmate commit ada21f5b.

Exit 255 means unknown, never failure

"An SSH exit status of 255 always means transport failure or unknown remote completion. The underlying fm-on transport never retries automatically, but fm-send retries its correlation-preserving steering-inbox leg exactly once. Semantic callers preserve the route or pending request; an operation that is not idempotent requires same-host reconciliation rather than a blind resend" docs/remote-secondmates.md:216-218. The transport's own header puts the responsibility on the caller: 255 "must be reconciled by the semantic caller, never blindly repeated by this layer" bin/fm-on.sh:18-21.

Why the distinction is real: the remote command may have completed after the connection dropped. On the far side, a caller that vanished cancels its staged job bin/fm-remote-entrypoint.sh:23-30, but a job that already published its result, or a side effect that already landed, is not undone. So 255 cannot mean "did not happen".

Unknown, not dead, and no failover

Three statements in the document fix the model. "An unreachable or unreadable remote read is unknown, not evidence that the endpoint is dead" docs/remote-secondmates.md:189. "An unavailable remote home is projected as unknown and is never replaced by a local second mate" docs/remote-secondmates.md:219. And Firstmate "does not support … failing a remote route over to a local replacement" docs/remote-secondmates.md:5.

The source's own reasons for keeping an unreachable endpoint as unknown:

  • The agent is designed to survive exactly this: its Herdr server belongs to the host's GUI login session, not the SSH connection, "so the agent's endpoint survives every disconnection the primary's supervision depends on" docs/remote-secondmates.md:10. A dropped link is therefore expected to leave a live mate behind.
  • A transport blip must not read as a dead or torn-down mate; "only the remote host's own dead/missing verdict may say the endpoint is actually gone" bin/fm-crew-state.sh:251-255.
Reading, not a quoted reason

A further consequence follows from the whole-home design but is not stated as the reason in the source: a local replacement would start a second owner of the same routed domain while the remote home, with its own backlog and workers, may still be running. The documents state the no-failover rule without spelling out this argument.

Liveness recovery at startup

An alive endpoint on a non-Herdr backend, or one whose route cannot be read, is skipped with an instruction to "migrate or retire it explicitly" rather than touched bin/fm-bootstrap.sh:776-789. The relaunch path goes through fm-spawn.sh, "so recovery passes the same readiness gate rather than a weaker one" docs/remote-secondmates.md:170. The sweep itself belongs to Supervision.

A remote route's parent metadata has no local spawn-generation marker and is identified by its recorded host. The Bearings inventory-reconcile hook therefore accepts these markerless routes, revalidates the sampled host at delivery, and refuses a route that changed hosts docs/remote-secondmates.md:172-173.

Other recovery paths in this area

  • Session takeover. When the guard replaces a foreign fm-remote server, every pane closes, and "the parent firstmate's secondmate liveness sweep relaunches its mates into the Aqua-born server" bin/fm-remote-herdr-guard.sh:33-34. See the guard.
  • Crashed job worker. Its running jobs are stopped and published as 125 by the next worker; an unconfirmed shutdown quarantines ownership until execution is proven stopped. See quarantine.
  • Abandoned caller. A disconnected fm-on cancels its job instead of leaving it to convoy retries. See cancellation.
  • Reply-log continuity break. Escalated as a keyed blocked: line; the source is not re-armed and the cursor is never silently reset bin/fm-procevent-remote-reply.sh:530-540.
  • Drifted host. "A launch after a host has drifted out of readiness fails with the doctor's own gap text instead of leaving a half-created endpoint" docs/remote-secondmates.md:166.
  • Endpoint in the wrong session. Classified unverified and left untouched until an operator migrates it docs/remote-secondmates.md:165.
Open: what "explicit migration" is

Several refusals say an endpoint must be "explicitly migrated" bin/fm-remote-secondmate-control.sh:90-111 docs/remote-secondmates.md:165. A search of bin/, docs/, and the skills finds no migration command or procedure for remote endpoints; the word appears only in these refusal messages. Whether it is a manual operator task or a command owned by another area is not settled here.
