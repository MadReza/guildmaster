Claude Stop auto-arm

On a Claude primary, watcher continuity is owned by a background Stop hook that fires on every turn end, arms one watcher cycle without spending a model token, and wakes the idle session with exit status 2 when that cycle produces an actionable wake.

Read against Firstmate commit ada21f5b. Up: Supervision. Script: bin/fm-claude-stop-autoarm.sh. Claim contract: bin/fm-wake-lib.sh:1405-1479.

Registration

.claude/settings.json registers two Stop hooks, both anchored at $CLAUDE_PROJECT_DIR: the synchronous guard bin/fm-turnend-guard.sh --claude, and bin/fm-claude-stop-autoarm.sh with "asyncRewake": true and a timeout of 28800 seconds (.claude/settings.json:43-48, docs/turnend-guard.md:79). Both commands start with [ -z "${GROK_AGENT:-}${GROK_HOOK_EVENT:-}" ] || exit 0, because Grok loads Claude-compatible settings and has no asyncRewake: an unguarded run once "waited on the foregrounded watcher for the declared 28800-second timeout and the Grok turn never ended" (docs/turnend-guard.md:89-93). A Cursor-delivered payload is refused for the same reason (bin/fm-claude-stop-autoarm.sh:115-121).

Gates, in order

"Claude Code fires it in the background on EVERY Stop of a Claude primary session, with no deduplication across firings" (bin/fm-claude-stop-autoarm.sh:5-7). Each firing passes these gates, and "On any uncertainty such as unresolvable ancestry, malformed lock state, or lock contention, it exits 0 and leaves continuity to the synchronous guard and the model" (bin/fm-claude-stop-autoarm.sh:71-75):

  1. Scope — a genuine primary checkout or validly marked secondmate home; child worktrees stay inert (bin/fm-claude-stop-autoarm.sh:123-124).
  2. Identity — this session owns state/.lock by the shared fm_session_lock_owned_by_self verdict; a numeric owner that fails the harness-liveness predicate is marked for guarded recovery, while a live foreign owner, missing, or malformed lock exits (bin/fm-claude-stop-autoarm.sh:126-140).
  3. Away — state/.afk present means the daemon owns the watcher; exit 0 and "NEVER rewake" (bin/fm-claude-stop-autoarm.sh:142-143).
  4. Need — fm_supervision_needed: task metadata, a registered process-event source, a registered custom check, or the Relay poll (bin/fm-supervision-lib.sh:47-105).
  5. Stale session-lock recovery — only now, after the away and need gates, delegate to bin/fm-lock.sh and re-verify ownership (bin/fm-claude-stop-autoarm.sh:151-158), "so an idle or away home remains byte-for-byte inert".
  6. Generation claim — below.

Generation claims in the epoch ledger

state/.claude-autoarm-epoch is the claim. Line 1 is epoch=N owner_pid=P outcome=O updated_at=T, plus session_pid=S recovery_generation=G on a rewake; line 2 is the claimant's mandatory process identity (bin/fm-wake-lib.sh:1411-1420). The rules:

  • A claim is open while its outcome is arming, its owner is alive, its recorded identity recomputes and matches, and it is not stuck — "both the ledger entry and the watcher beacon … are older than the guard grace, which proves the owner hung mid-arm with nothing supervising" (bin/fm-wake-lib.sh:1421-1427, bin/fm-wake-lib.sh:1530-1548).
  • Every firing defers (exit 0) to an open claim; anything else lets the firing take generation N+1. "Taking a newer generation IS the reclaim: a steady-state predecessor is never signalled or revoked" (bin/fm-wake-lib.sh:1428-1432).
  • "NO mutex is ever held across a blocking step"; state/.claude-autoarm.lock survives only as a micro-mutex around individual ledger writes (bin/fm-wake-lib.sh:1433-1437).
  • "A superseded owner goes COMPLETELY silent … Ownership is re-verified before every side effect" (bin/fm-wake-lib.sh:1438-1440); the arm loop checks fm_autoarm_still_owner before each attempt (bin/fm-claude-stop-autoarm.sh:263-270).
  • "The irrevocable commit point of a translation is the EXIT STATUS: the harness delivers the collected stderr banner only on exit 2 and discards it on exit 0" — so a losing generation that already printed a banner simply exits 0 and nothing is delivered (bin/fm-wake-lib.sh:1441-1448, bin/fm-claude-stop-autoarm.sh:185-208).

Why: this "structurally removes the failure classes the lock-held-across-arm design produced": a hook that "hung mid-arm with its ledger frozen at 'arming' kept the watcher from ever being auto-re-armed again" (2026-08-26), and "a finished claim whose leftover lock silenced both participants for 40 beacon-less minutes" (2026-08-14) (bin/fm-wake-lib.sh:1450-1457).

The foreground arm and its translation

The winning owner runs bin/fm-watch-arm.sh in the foreground of the hook-owned process tree, exporting its resolved FM_GUARD_GRACE so arm and watcher judge staleness with the same value: "NO shell &: this hook process tree is the harness-owned lifecycle" (bin/fm-claude-stop-autoarm.sh:252-277). The grace defaults to max(300, FM_POLL + 60), the fix for a long-poll home whose healthy watcher read stale at the edge of every cycle (bin/fm-claude-stop-autoarm.sh:103-108, docs/turnend-guard.md:69-73).

  • Actionable close (signal:, stale:, check:, heartbeat): print a banner to stderr — "one supervision event needs a handling turn now", up to eight reason lines, and the instruction to drain first, acknowledge after handling, and "do NOT run bin/fm-watch-arm.sh after an ordinary wake" — then commit rewake and exit 2 (bin/fm-claude-stop-autoarm.sh:341-359). Claude delivers it as "Stop hook feedback", which wakes the session even while idle (bin/fm-claude-stop-autoarm.sh:44-47).
  • Non-actionable close with a healthy watcher: benign; reset the failure episode and record clean (bin/fm-claude-stop-autoarm.sh:293-331).
  • Need vanished mid-cycle (fleet torn down): clean, exit 0 (bin/fm-claude-stop-autoarm.sh:304-310).
  • Exhausted failure: one notice per episode — "the Stop-owned automatic supervision mechanism is broken … Do not launch a manual background arm from this notice" — then exit 2 so the next Stop retries (bin/fm-claude-stop-autoarm.sh:361-388).
  • Host interruption: HUP, TERM, or INT from Claude's timeout or teardown are translated "through the ordinary durable failure protocol instead of leaving the generation frozen at arming" (bin/fm-claude-stop-autoarm.sh:216-245).

The rewake outcome records the session-lock pid and the current recovery-marker generation. That binding is what lets the mid-turn pull guard stay quiet when a long handling turn outlives the beacon grace: fm_autoarm_midturn_healthy accepts a stale beacon only while the latest outcome is a rewake for the live session and the same recovery generation, newer than the last beacon, with no failure marker (bin/fm-wake-lib.sh:1550-1582).

Accepted residuals

Two are stated as "ACCEPTED INTENT, because closing them absolutely would require a mutex held across output or steady-state revocation, both deliberately rejected": an owner that dies between its terminal write and its exit (a committed outcome whose banner never arrives), and a hung old-build owner resuming during the one upgrade window. "Each residual costs at most one extra exit-2 continuation turn absorbed by the durable idempotent wake queue" (bin/fm-wake-lib.sh:1459-1470). A legacy lock-holding claim from a pre-generation build is handled by a shim that defers while it is genuinely deciding and reclaims it once when proven abandoned, retiring a verified live owner with TERM (bin/fm-wake-lib.sh:1472-1479, bin/fm-wake-lib.sh:1698-1817).

Cooperation with the turn-end guard

The guard fires on the same Stop, synchronously. It waits up to FM_CLAUDE_AUTOARM_SYNC_WAIT_MS (800 ms) for proof that the auto-arm owns recovery — a healthy watcher, an open claim, or a fresh rewake — "so one event epoch yields exactly one recovery turn" (bin/fm-turnend-guard.sh:60-91). The budget, the one attended fail-open, and how the two hooks share the failure markers are on Turn-end and pull guards.

Where it is pinned

tests/fm-claude-stop-autoarm.test.sh covers scope, stale and live owners, away and need boundaries, single-flight, bounded retries, one-notice episodes, exit-2 translation, and HUP/TERM/INT translation; an opt-in live end-to-end test runs two tokenless cycles against a real Claude (docs/watcher-continuity.md:127-129).
