Supervision

The loop that keeps the first mate aware of its fleet while it is not looking, and the stack of mechanisms that stop any supervisor turn from ending with work in flight and nobody watching.

Read against Firstmate commit ada21f5b. Up: Level 2 containers, where the watcher, the wait and turn-end hooks, the away sub-supervisor, and the durable wake queue appear as containers. This section opens them to Level 3.

The problem this area solves

The first mate is a language-model session. It only acts during a turn, and a turn only starts when something delivers input to it. Every worker it supervises runs in another terminal and finishes, blocks, or wedges on its own schedule. Supervision is therefore the answer to two questions that the model cannot answer for itself:

  1. Who notices, between turns, that a worker needs attention? A bash poll loop, bin/fm-watch.sh, classifies every candidate event for free and only wakes the model for the ones that need judgment (bin/fm-watch.sh:247-265).
  2. Who guarantees that such a loop is running at every moment work is in flight? Not the model's memory. Per-harness hooks and extensions re-arm the loop at turn boundaries, and a turn-end guard blocks or forces a follow-up whenever a turn would end with no live watcher (docs/watcher-continuity.md:3-4, docs/turnend-guard.md:12-19).

Everything else in this section exists to make those two answers survive crashes, interrupted turns, restarted sessions, duplicate processes, and the quirks of eight different agent harnesses. AGENTS.md:418-456 states the operating rule the supervisor follows: keep exactly one live supervision cycle, drain the queue before anything else on a wake, and acknowledge only after handling.

Invariants the whole area defends

Exactly one live cycle per home
The watcher is a singleton through state/.watch.lock; a duplicate stands down within one poll by self-eviction (bin/fm-watch.sh:2296-2305). Repair is home-scoped; pkill -f bin/fm-watch.sh is forbidden because it would kill sibling homes (AGENTS.md:452-453, bin/fm-watch-arm.sh:54-60).
Absorb only on positive evidence
A wake is silently absorbed only when the crew is provably working or has declared a bounded wait; every uncertain case surfaces (bin/fm-watch.sh:5-18).
Enqueue before suppress, acknowledge after handling
A durable row exists before any marker says "already reported", and it is deleted only by an explicit post-handling acknowledgement (AGENTS.md:433, bin/fm-supervise-daemon.sh:35-40).
No turn ends blind
At every primary turn boundary with supervision need, a live watcher (or, while away, a live daemon) with a fresh beacon must exist, or the harness is made to continue (docs/turnend-guard.md:14-19).
Never an automatic interrupt
Wedge escalation is for human inspection only; the watcher never signals, interrupts, or restarts a worker (bin/fm-watch.sh:73-78).
Waiting is silent
Empty polls, absorbed wakes, and elapsed time are never captain-facing (AGENTS.md:451).

Where this area touches its siblings

  • Communication owns status-line grammar, the steering inbox and its re-ring ladder, and operational input prefixes. Supervision only calls the inbox ladder once per window per poll (bin/fm-watch.sh:434-510).
  • Persistence owns the layout of state/ and the session lock state/.lock.
  • Crew owns spawning, harness adapters, and backends, including where each harness's busy hooks are installed.
  • Delivery owns PR merge polls and no-mistakes runs; the watcher only executes their checks and reads run-step state.
  • Backlog and decisions owns captain holds and the decision fold that the drain's OPEN DECISIONS section prints.
  • Prompts and skills owns the /afk and /quiet skills and how bin/fm-supervision-instructions.sh renders docs/supervision-protocols/ into the session-start block.
  • Observability owns fleet views; supervision's own diagnostics are state/.watch-triage.log, state/.watch-cycle-exits.log, and state/.watch-deliveries.log.
  • Remote owns remote secondmate homes, which keep their host-local supervision boundary.
