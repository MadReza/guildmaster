Briefs as executable contracts

A brief is the complete written instruction set one worker receives. Firstmate generates most of it from scripts, leaves exactly two holes for the supervisor to fill, and refuses to launch a worker whose brief was filled wrongly: "The scaffold is a safety contract, not a suggestion." AGENTS.md:567

Read against Firstmate commit ada21f5b. Up: Prompts and skills. The status protocol and steering inbox a brief describes are owned by Communication; delivery modes and merge authority by Delivery.

How a scout brief differs

The scout scaffold keeps the Task, Herdr, rules, and inbox sections, but its Setup declares the worktree "your laboratory - install, run, edit, and make scratch commits freely; all of it is discarded at teardown. The report is the only thing that survives" bin/fm-brief.sh:417-420. Rule 1 becomes "Never push to any remote and never open a PR" bin/fm-brief.sh:423. Its definition of done asks for a self-contained data/<id>/report.md with evidence and recommendations, the captain-hold-lifecycle completion gate, and notes that "firstmate may promote this task in place" bin/fm-brief.sh:462-468. It offers the Lavish board only when fm-bootstrap.sh lavish-compatible confirms the tool floor, otherwise a text report bin/fm-brief.sh:403-408. A scout brief never carries a delivery mode: --mode on a scout "is refused" bin/fm-brief.sh:55-56.

The split: captain's intent versus Firstmate spec

Every ship and scout brief's # Task has two holes, and they are filled under different rules.

Why the reviewer treats intent as acceptance criteria

AGENTS.md gives the reason in the same sentence as the rule: never widen the intent "because the reviewer treats that subsection as acceptance criteria" AGENTS.md:555. The reviewer is the no-mistakes pipeline, and the channel is its --intent argument, whose worker-side contract fm-dod-lib.sh owns: "When starting no-mistakes, pass --intent as only this brief's ## Captain's intent subsection body, not its heading, plus any later words the captain actually said … Do not include ## Firstmate spec, later Firstmate build constraints, or your own decisions and tradeoffs." bin/fm-dod-lib.sh:285-289

Read together, the source gives three reasons for keeping that string narrow:

  1. Scope follows the string. Whatever is in --intent becomes what the pipeline holds the change to. A Firstmate-authored generalization placed there would turn into findings against the worker for work the captain never asked for, which is exactly the "follow-up work to note, not scope to add" that section 11 forbids AGENTS.md:556.
  2. The external default is the opposite. Firstmate overrides the no-mistakes skill's own guidance: "This replaces the no-mistakes skill's advice to enrich --intent with decisions and tradeoffs; that advice does not apply to Firstmate-dispatched work." bin/fm-dod-lib.sh:292
  3. Firstmate decides findings against the same contract. When a gate raises an ask-user finding, ask-user-authority reconstructs "the accepted contract from the brief's ## Captain's intent subsection, later captain words, and the specification in ## Firstmate spec and steers. Reviewer language cannot amend that contract." .agents/skills/ask-user-authority/SKILL.md:25-26 A finding that "would materially expand the contract" escalates to the captain .agents/skills/ask-user-authority/SKILL.md:32-33. If the intent were padded, expansions would look like in-scope corrections and never reach the captain.

The string must also stand alone: "that string plus the codebase must let a reader reconstruct roughly the same specification, without depending on a separate report, a PR, or context that lives only in this conversation", so "do items 1, 2, 3, and 7 of the report" must be written out as the substance of those items bin/fm-dod-lib.sh:290-291.

Outside Firstmate's source

How no-mistakes itself uses --intent inside its review is not in this repository; Firstmate only states that the reviewer treats it as acceptance criteria. The claim above rests on AGENTS.md, fm-dod-lib.sh, and ask-user-authority, not on no-mistakes' implementation.

Provenance without labels

The heading, not a prefix, records who said it: "Author the subsection body and later relays as the actual words, without adding speaker labels or direct address: the heading supplies provenance and is not part of --intent." bin/fm-dod-lib.sh:20-22 Spawn enforces it: a ## Captain's intent line that opens with Captain:, Captain's words:, Captain's ask:, Captain's intent:, or Captain, is refused bin/fm-dod-lib.sh:223-228. Older briefs with one mixed # Task are accepted only when captain lines are marked [captain] ; for a no-mistakes ship with no marked words the worker must "stop and ask firstmate instead of starting no-mistakes" bin/fm-dod-lib.sh:287-288. The heading-based design also matches AGENTS.md's channel bound, which forbids direct address in any non-chat artifact "such as a commit message, PR or issue description, brief, code, or comment" AGENTS.md:13.

Definitions of done

Each ship DoD opens with a fixed machine-readable line, Delivery contract: mode=<mode>, that spawn checks against its own --mode bin/fm-dod-lib.sh:11-12.

Two rules in the no-mistakes block are about the harness rather than the pipeline: "Claude Code kills a command at ten minutes maximum, while one fix round is capped around thirty minutes and up to three rounds chain. So background the drive call and poll" bin/fm-dod-lib.sh:295-296, and "A killed or timed-out call is never evidence the daemon died" bin/fm-dod-lib.sh:298. Both exist because a worker would otherwise misreport a live run as blocked. --yes is "banned fleet-wide" because "answering your own ask-user finding is a hard rule violation" bin/fm-dod-lib.sh:305-306.

What a brief deliberately never carries is merge authority: "There is no --yolo flag here. The worker never owns merge decisions, so yolo is a spawn-time and firstmate-side input only" bin/fm-brief.sh:57-58, and passing --yolo to the scaffold is an error so it is "never silently dropped here and then believed to have been recorded" bin/fm-brief.sh:166-169.

Home brief include

config/brief-include.md lets one home add standing worker instructions "without editing this tracked script" bin/fm-brief.sh:78-79. It is local, gitignored, not inherited by secondmate homes, and AGENTS.md tells the supervisor to "keep its text out of ## Firstmate spec" AGENTS.md:85. It goes last and yields to everything above it, secondmate charters never take it, and it may not declare a delivery mode because "a later scout promotion could not outrank" it bin/fm-brief.sh:80-88.
