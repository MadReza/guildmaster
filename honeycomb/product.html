The core analysis loop

Honeycomb's docs describe one workflow behind every feature: see something odd on a graph, select it, let BubbleUp say what is different about it, and then filter or group by that and look again, down to single traces documented docs-core-loop. Majors's 2017 advice explains why this needs raw, wide events: "Any aggregation that is performed at write time is actively harmful to your ability to understand the health and experience of each request" wb-best-practices.

The query builder

A query is SELECT (labelled VISUALIZE until recently), WHERE, GROUP BY, ORDER BY, LIMIT and HAVING docs-query-build. Its results are a graph per calculation and a table per group, and both are kept: "Honeycomb persists query results forever, so clicking into past queries is super fast" talk/blog blog-query-history. A permalink keeps working after the underlying data has aged out blog-infinite-retention. The data model page lists every operator.

Heatmaps and BubbleUp

A heatmap shows what a percentile line hides: here the median barely moves, but a second population of requests near a second and a half appears for twenty minutes. Drawing a box around it starts BubbleUp: "BubbleUp computes the values of all dimensions both inside the box (the anomaly you care about and want to explain) and outside the box (the baseline), and then compares the two and sorts the resulting view by percent of differences" documented docs-core-loop.

  • BubbleUp came out of beta in early 2019 blog-bubbleup-2019; since October 2022 it starts from any query, a line on a grouped chart or a results row, not only a heatmap blog-bubbleup-2022.
  • The scoring formula is not published: "sorts ... by percent of differences" is all the docs say docs-core-loop. Its designer described "five distinct code paths" for drawing different kinds of fields fisher-bar-charts.
  • Its blind spot is high-cardinality fields: "dimensions with very high cardinality, like user ID, may not have a meaningful 'baseline' to establish difference from, so they may not be ranked highly". AI-written insights, added in 2026 for teams with Honeycomb Intelligence, summarise the strongest differences talk/blog blog-ai-bubbleup.
  • Agents can run it too, through the MCP server's run_bubbleup tool blog-mcp-ga docs-mcp-tools.

Traces

The trace view shows a summary of up to six levels of span dependency, the waterfall, and a sidebar of each span's fields, span events and links; rows are coloured by service.name when a trace crosses services documented docs-explore-traces. A span with gen_ai.operation.name gets a Gen AI tab docs-explore-traces. Relational fields such as root.http.route make queries trace-aware docs-query-build. The data model page draws one trace as events.

Service Map

It launched in October 2022 and became Enterprise-only after that year blog-service-map. Internally a separate service consumes the ingest stream and records the edges talk/blog blog-service-map-iter. Honeycomb's advice is to keep service.name deliberately coarse, "the identity you want on the map", and put pods and components in other attributes docs-service-map-how.

SLOs and burn alerts

An SLI is a calculated field that, "for each event in the Dataset, ... returns true (success), false (failure), or null (not applicable)", usually written IF(qualifier, criterion); the SLO is a target proportion over a rolling window of days, and the error budget is counted in events documented docs-sli docs-slos. Burn alerts come in two kinds: exhaustion time, "at risk of burning through its error budget within a specified number of hours", and budget rate, a drop of at least some percentage within a window docs-slo-monitor.

  • Event-based, not window-based. "A bad minute where 50% of the events fail but only four events were seen won't alert you" talk/blog blog-budget-rate; the flip side is volume: "an SLO of 99.9% needs a minimum level of traffic of a few tens of thousands of events a day to be meaningful" blog-slo-challenges.
  • Built the expensive way first. The first alert loop re-queried at one-minute resolution every minute "and promptly arranged a $10,000 day of AWS spend on data retrieval"; the fix was a cache that never stores approximate results blog-slo-challenges. In 2021 the computation moved to Beagle, a stream processor blog-beagle.
  • Budget-rate alerts arrived in November 2023 because exhaustion alerts went silent "once an organization's budget hit 0%" blog-budget-rate.
  • Limits. Events more than 25 hours late are ignored docs-slo-create; one SLO per SLI; up to 10 services share one budget docs-slo-practice; time-series metrics cannot back an SLO docs-metrics-faq. SLOs were Enterprise-only in 2020; Pro now includes two wb-pricing-2020 pricing.

Triggers

A trigger is a scheduled query with a threshold: "a trigger with a duration of 5 minutes and a frequency of 2 minutes will check the last 5 minutes of data every 2 minutes" documented docs-triggers.
